Can 32 numbers recognise a footballer?

I described every player in Europe's top five leagues with 32 numbers about how they play — then hid their names. Given only the first half of their 2017/18 season, could a model pick out the same player in the second half?

Often, yes. And the honest part is showing exactly when it can't.

The idea in 30 seconds

Like recognising a friend by how they walk.

If the way a player passes, carries and defends is personal enough, their numbers should look alike in October and in April — even though the opponents changed.

01

Split the season in two

Aug–Jan becomes the "before", Jan–May the "after". Only players with 450+ minutes in each half count.

02

Describe each half with 32 numbers

Passing, progression, chance creation, shooting, defending, position on the pitch… no names, no clubs.

03

Ask: who looks most alike?

Rank all 1,257 "after" profiles by similarity to one "before". The higher the real player lands, the stronger the fingerprint.

The bottom line Players do leave an individual trace

But teammates and club context carry a lot of it. The finding is narrower than the headline — and the site says so up front.

Reading it right

This project shows
How a player acts carries a measurable, individual signal.
That signal holds across two halves of a season and on a second data provider.
Every number is reproducible from frozen, openly licensed data.
It does not claim
That two similar players share a playing style.
That a statistical neighbour is a signing or replacement recommendation.
Anything about future performance, tactical fit or transfer value.
